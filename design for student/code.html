The Schrödinger's Cat Paradox

The Schrödinger's cat thought experiment was devised in 1935 to illustrate the problem of the Copenhagen interpretation of quantum mechanics when applied to everyday objects. Here is a step-by-step breakdown:

1. The Setup

Imagine a cat placed in a sealed steel box, along with a Geiger counter, a vial of poison, and a radioactive source.

2. The Trigger

If the radioactive substance decays, the Geiger counter detects it and releases the poison, killing the cat.

3. The Superposition

According to quantum mechanics, the radioactive atom is in a superposition—both decayed and not decayed—until observed. Therefore, the cat is simultaneously dead and alive.

Mathematical Representation content_copy
|ψ⟩ = 1/√2 (|alive⟩ + |dead⟩)

The paradox highlights the "measurement problem": at what point does a quantum system stop being a mixture of states and become one or the other?
